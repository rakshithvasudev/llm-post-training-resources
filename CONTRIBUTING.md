# Contributing

This list is meant to stay short enough to actually read. A good addition replaces something weaker, or fills a real gap.

## What belongs here

- Primary sources: the paper, the official technical report, the original blog post, or the canonical repository.
- Work that explains a mechanism, gives a reproducible recipe, or reports a lesson that changed how people train models.
- Secondary explainers only when they are clearly the best explanation of a topic available.

## What doesn't

- Announcements, leaderboards, and marketing posts.
- Papers whose main contribution is a benchmark number with no insight into why.
- Aggregator sites, AI-generated summaries, and paywalled reposts of open content.

## Format

One line per entry, in the section where a reader would look for it:

```markdown
- [Exact title](https://link) - One sentence on what the reader will learn from it.
```

- Use the exact title of the work, including any subtitle. A widely used short name may follow in parentheses, e.g. "(InstructGPT)". Link arXiv papers as `https://arxiv.org/abs/<id>`.
- Describe the idea, not the hype. "Clips the importance weight instead of the update" is useful; "a groundbreaking new method" is not.
- Within a section, keep chronological order (by arXiv ID, or publication date for non-arXiv work) unless the section intro says otherwise.
- Each work gets one full entry, in the section where it fits best. Elsewhere, point to it instead of repeating it. Sections 0 and 19 are curated paths and may repeat entries.
- New, unproven work goes in the Frontier section first.

Open a pull request with a sentence on why the resource earns its place.
